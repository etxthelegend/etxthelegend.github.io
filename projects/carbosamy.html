CarboSAMY

Automated deep-learning segmentation of soot in TEM images

Year
2026
Context
UBC Energy & Aerosol Laboratory
Tools
Python · Deep learning · Image processing · TEM
IMECE 2026 poster (PDF)

A deep-learning workflow that finds, segments, and measures soot and other carbonaceous particles in electron-microscope images, reducing analysis time by 99.6%.

Why

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) is the standard way to see the structure of soot aggregates, but measuring them means hand-tracing particles image by image. That slow, subjective step limits how much data a study can use. CarboSAMY automates it end to end and cuts analysis time by 99.6%.

The model

How CarboSAMY turns a raw TEM image into measured particles.

1

Architecture

A µSAM segmentation model fine-tuned on TEM images and their hand-traced masks, prompted by a YOLO detector that finds and classifies each particle.

2

Inference workflow

The same workflow runs at two levels: CarboSAMY-agg segments whole aggregates, and CarboSAMY-pp extracts and sizes the primary particles inside them.

3

Input and output

Validation and results

I coordinated a small annotation team that hand-segmented images as ground truth, reviewing consistency, particle identification, and mask quality. CarboSAMY was then compared with manual analysis and existing methods.

1

Aggregate segmentation

Mean IoU of 93% on typical soot, matching the Carboseg benchmark and well above k-means, and 89% on difficult lacey-carbon grids. Aggregate diameters track manual measurements with under 1% bias.

2

Primary-particle sizing

Primary-particle size bias against manual analysis of +11%, compared with +40% for the circular Hough transform (CHT) and +57% for SAGE.
