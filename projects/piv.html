Particle Image Velocimetry over an Airfoil

Measuring flow separation and circulation on a NACA 0012

Year
2025
Context
UBC Mechanical Engineering
Tools
PIV · Experimental fluids · Aerodynamics

PIV in a recirculating water channel to map the flow around a NACA 0012 from ~1° to ~38° angle of attack, and estimate lift from circulation.

Motivation

How flow behaves around an airfoil decides lift, stall, and wake development. PIV lets us see separation and vortex formation directly and also put numbers on them: integrating vorticity gives circulation, a proxy for lift.

Goals

  • Measure velocity fields at angles of attack from about 1.3° to 37.7°
  • Compute vorticity and circulation as a lift proxy
  • Compare laminar and jet inflow conditions
  • Observe separation and wake growth as angle of attack increases

Setup

A 48-inch acrylic recirculating water trough, seeded with coffee grounds and lit from below by a lightboard. Perforated screens and an inverted-V baffle conditioned the inflow, and a Nikon D750 recorded particle motion for each test condition.

Results

  • Captured attached, transitional, and fully separated flow
  • Wake grew with angle of attack, as expected
  • Circulation increased with angle of attack, consistent with aerodynamic theory

Error sources

Non-uniform inflow from the trough design, seed particles avoiding the free surface, ripples and optical distortion, large seed particles limiting resolution, and blockage near the airfoil.
