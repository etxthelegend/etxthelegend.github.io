Smart Brake Chamber

Anti-theft caging-port protection for trailer air brakes

Year
2025–26
Context
Capstone Team 12, BrakeIn Security · sponsored by Level5Fleet
Tools
SolidWorks · Engineering drawings · FEA · Tolerance analysis · Solenoid actuation · Rapid prototyping

A motorized locking device that keeps thieves from releasing a trailer's parking brakes through the caging port, while staying quick to remove for authorized maintenance.

Problem

Trailer theft costs the trucking industry millions every year. A common method defeats the air-brake system by pressurizing the spring brake chamber or reaching the caging port to mechanically release the parking brakes. Our system targets three attack paths: pressurizing the supply line, uncontrolled venting, and access to the caging port. My work focused on the caging port.

Requirements

  • Block direct access to the caging port in normal operation
  • Survive at least 10 minutes of angle grinding and drilling
  • Unlock and come off quickly for authorized service
  • Fit existing brake chambers without modifying their internals
  • Stay cheap enough for fleet-wide deployment

Design

I went through 10+ SolidWorks iterations before settling on the final locking-plug assembly. A locking plug closes off the caging port and is held in place by two solenoid-driven actuator plugs, so the port can only be opened electronically. A custom PCB enclosure inside the brake housing holds the control electronics, a DC-DC buck converter, and a 2.4 GHz antenna for wireless control. The assembly bolts to the parking-brake housing with M5 class 10.9 hardware and an oil-resistant seal.

Drawings & analysis

I co-authored the full drawing package: a 15-item bill of materials, the exploded assembly, and detail drawings for every custom part, including the housing, mounting plate and brackets, locking plug, actuator plugs, and electronics enclosure. Tolerance stack-ups and FEA checked fit, manufacturability, and the strength of critical parts.

Testing

Early 3D-printed prototypes with motors and actuators were mounted on a brake-chamber mock-up and cycled through repeated lock/unlock tests, then the design moved to full prototype chambers powered from a 12 V supply. The device locked and unlocked reliably, fully covered the port, and met 100% of its functional tests, with minor alignment refinements identified.

Showcase

The team presented the final design and working prototype at the UBC capstone showcase.

Next steps

  • Metal prototype in hardened steel
  • Standardized 10-minute tamper tests
  • Integrate with supply-line protection and venting control
